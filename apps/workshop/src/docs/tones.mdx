# Tones

A tone is **what something means**, never what colour it is. "Critical" is a rule
someone can follow; "red" isn't.

<ToneMatrix />

## The set

Current tones, with the framing borrowed from Braid — defined by risk and urgency
rather than by appearance:

| tone       | when to reach for it                                                                            | risk / urgency |
| ---------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------- |
| `neutral`  | the default. Subtle information, historic or archived detail, actions with no particular weight | none           |
| `accent`   | the emphasised action or the thing you want chosen                                              | —              |
| `critical` | errors, failures, destructive actions. "You need to see this"                                   | high / high    |
| `warning`  | something mid-process or needing attention before it becomes a problem                          | low / low      |
| `success`  | completion, positive confirmation                                                               | —              |

**Open:** Braid's set is `critical`, `caution`, `positive`, `neutral`, `info`,
`promote`, and their names are better than ours — `caution` and `positive` describe
a situation, where `warning` and `success` describe an outcome. Adopting theirs
would also add `info` (calm guidance from the UI) and `promote` (things being given
visibility). Not yet decided.

## Not every component gets every tone

A tone palette is system-wide, but each component exposes only the subset that
makes sense for it. `info` and `promote` are things a Badge or an Alert can be;
they aren't things an _action_ can be. Button therefore offers fewer tones than a
status component will.

This is deliberate: uniform tone lists across every component look consistent and
produce nonsense like a "promote" submit button.

## Getting a brand colour in

Brand enters as a **primitive ramp**, not as a special case. One hex from the
client, twelve generated steps, and the semantic accent tokens point at it. Every
component that already understands tones is then correct in that brand, with no
component changes — which is the whole reason the ramp shape is fixed.

The complication worth planning for: **a brand colour is often not a usable UI
colour.** Brand palettes are chosen for logos and marketing, and they routinely
fail contrast at step 9 (solid fills) or step 11 (text). If the only accent is the
brand hue, you either break accessibility or render the brand wrong.

Braid's answer is to have two accents, and it's the right one:

|                  | what it's for                                                                                                        |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **brand accent** | hero moments, marketing surfaces, the literal brand colour. Used sparingly                                           |
| **form accent**  | the everyday interactive colour — the emphasised action in a form, focus rings, links. Contrast-safe by construction |

Usually both point at the same ramp. When a brand hue can't carry UI weight, the
form accent points at a nearby hue that can, and only the brand accent stays
literally on-brand. Client themes then differ by one or two ramp declarations
rather than by a pile of per-component overrides.

**Open:** whether to split `accent` into brand and form accents now or when a
second client actually exists.
